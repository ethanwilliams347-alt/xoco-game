## What V19 is

Build a `CnC_parallax`-shaped scene — seven depth bands, deliberately cheap
generated art, and a **ground plane doing what the reference's water does** —
before the underground-cave split-view path. Full entry at `V19` in
[ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md); measurements at
[reference_observations.txt](reference_observations.txt) entries 7 and 8.

**Seven reference bands do not mean seven new layers.** Working the mapping out
was most of the planning:

| reference band | ours |
|---|---|
| sky | `backdrop_sky`, exists |
| far range | **new** |
| mid range | `backdrop_mountains`, exists, at 0.60 against a reference-implied 0.63 |
| near ridge | **new** — lands in the band V11 built and deleted |
| shore treeline | **new** — same band |
| ground plane | **new, and the item's centre of gravity** |
| near silhouette | **already filled by the simulated world.** Do not author one. |

That last row is a refusal, not an omission. The reference's foreground rock is
a painting the boat passes behind; ours is diggable terrain, and **a painted band
in front of the world would occlude the one verb the game has.**

**Art is cheap here on purpose** — one colour, one or two shades, rough
silhouettes, generated by `tools/generate_backdrop.py` the way sky and mountains
already are. Generated layers conform to `PALETTE` by construction, which is the
property the 2026-08-12 palette deferral deliberately kept for this half of the
pipeline. Nothing here needs a drawn asset, so nothing here waits on V12.

## The gate is passed — do not re-ask it

V11 deleted a mid-ground band on 2026-08-16 and wrote down the reopen trigger:

```
region            rows      non-background    background L
upper sky        60- 250          0.7%          18 - 35
mid band        250- 470         30.3%          18 - 35
low band        470- 588         51.0%          18 - 112
ground plane    600-1000          0.5%          22.7 flat
```

The mid band is **70% bare sky**. The near ridge and the shore treeline have
somewhere to go. Entry in `PLAYTEST_LOG.md`, symptoms only.

**Two things that frame found which nobody was looking for**, and they change
what 4b is for:

- **The ground plane has a luminance spread of exactly 0.0** across 400 rows and
  37% of the frame. Not a flat band — a flat fill.
- **The ground plane and the upper sky are the same value**: 22.70 against 22.3.
  Four tenths of a level out of 255 between the nearest surface in the frame and
  one of the most distant. Same shape as the sky-versus-mountains reading that
  bought the 0.60 grade in step 3, on a much larger pair of surfaces, and step
  3's measurement could not have caught it because it only compared the two
  backdrop bands to each other.

## What 4a built (`76a3d30`) — the no-op half

**The user chose tiled**, so V16's wrapping layers are pulled forward into V19
rather than left scheduled behind it. The reason is arithmetic, not taste:
`layer_size()` in `tools/generate_backdrop.py` makes a non-wrapping layer as wide
as the window plus the whole pan range at its own parallax factor, so **the
nearer the band, the bigger its file** — sky 16 MB, mountains 20 MB, and every
new band sits nearer than either. Priced flat, five of them is roughly three
times the whole of `assets/` for art that is one repeating texture.

- **`src/render/backdrop_wrap.h`** — pure arithmetic, no SDL. `wrap_axis(origin,
  tile, window)` turns an unbounded parallax origin into a `first` offset in
  `(-tile, 0]` and a minimal copy `count`.
- **`tests/test_backdrop.cpp`** — a thirteenth suite, **linking no source set at
  all**, the narrowest link line in the project. Two properties sweep 120,000
  origins; the named cases above them all pass against implementations that are
  wrong where nobody looks. Verified against three mutations before being kept.
- **Nothing calls it, so the golden checksum did not move.** Still
  `0x9d9e92a81c4df07b`. Fourth use of the house procedure.

## Next step — 4b, the ground plane

**This is the visible half, and it is the first thing in V19 that moves the
checksum.** Do it before the other three bands: it is the item's centre of
gravity, it is the band the measurement above indicts, and it is the one that
proves the wrapping draw path that the other three will then reuse for free.

Roughly, and in this order:

1. **A wrapping draw in `src/render/frame.cpp`** — a sibling to
   `draw_backdrop_layer` that calls `wrap_axis` per axis and issues the copies.
   Keep `apply_grade` unconditional on it for the reason the existing helper
   states: colour mod lives on the texture, not the draw call.
2. **A tiled ground-plane generator** in `tools/generate_backdrop.py`, plus its
   row in `LAYERS` so `--header` and `--sizes` both see it. **A wrapping layer's
   size is not `layer_size()`** — that function encodes the very relationship
   being retired, so a wrapping row must not be sized through it. Say so in the
   script, because the next reader will assume otherwise.
3. **A row in the layer table**, between `mountains` and `props`, **with a grade
   of its own.** Identity is the wrong default here: the whole finding above is
   that this surface has no separation from the sky.
4. **Prove the mechanism at identity against `0x9d9e92a81c4df07b` first**, then
   let the grade move it. That is the house rule and it has been used four times;
   the new checksum goes in the **same commit** as the change, because `git log
   -S` on the constant is how the frame's history is read.

**Then: the Manual Tester Checklist is owed and you cannot run it.** Steps **11**
(parallax ordering and the pan-limit seam — the layer table changes shape *and* a
wrapping layer is a new way to produce a seam, so this is the step that matters
most) and **12** (the grade, watching for over-correction). Say so; do not quote
the golden test as covering it — **it hashes a software rasterisation and is blind
to a GPU-only defect.**

### After 4b

The far range, the near ridge and the shore treeline — three more rows reusing
the same draw path, each one colour and a shade. Then the value ladder from entry
7 is what the grades get tuned against:

```
band              L      x band behind    x sky
sky            173.6         -            1.00
far range      129.2        0.74          0.74
mid range      110.2        0.85          0.63
near ridge      80.7        0.73          0.46
shore treeline  63.5        0.79          0.37
ground plane   77.5 -> 138.2
foreground rock 51.6        0.81          0.30
```

**The ground plane is the row that breaks the ladder, and that is the mechanism
worth copying.** Every other band is a single value; the plane runs 77.5 at its
far edge to 138.2 at its near one. It is the only surface in the reference that
*reads as receding within itself*, and that is where the into-the-page effect
comes from — not from the count of layers.

### The one decision deliberately left open

**Whether the world row takes a grade below the plane's.** In the reference the
plane is *brighter* than what stands on it; ours runs the other way with the
world at 1.0. Grading the world down is coherent — grade multiplies and light
adds, so fire lights it back up — but it changes how the play area reads while
digging. **That is a `TUNING.md` row and a playtest, not an implementation
detail.** Do not settle it inside a commit that is about something else.
