Art Direction (V5)
===================

Deliverable for ROADMAP.md's V5. Written because V5 said, in its own words,
that everything after it — V3, V4, V6, V7-rest, V8 — is authored *against*
this document, and that drifting per-asset is worse than picking either
direction in writing. This picks one.

THE FINDING THIS RESOLVES
--------------------------

Three references existed and none had been chosen over the others:
notes/art_pipeline.txt's snowy Japanese level-crossing stills (written to
justify one test fixture and never actually adopted), notes/story.txt's
dystopian United States, and now resources/video_screenshots/test_location.jpg
— a screenshot of Noita's own opening forest biome at night.

The reconciliation V5 itself proposed is the one this takes: story.txt's
"quantum worlds" are explicitly *other* worlds, reached from a home base that
is the dystopian US. A destination world does not have to look like the home
base. So:

  - The dystopian US (story.txt) is the frame story and the home base. It is
    not a quantum world and nothing in this document touches it.
  - The Noita-forest reference is adopted as the first quantum world's
    direction. It replaces the crossing stills as the thing V3/V4/V6/V8 are
    authored against, because the crossing reference was never more than a
    placeholder that outlived its justification.
  - The crossing stills are retired from art direction. Nothing is deleted;
    they simply stop being what new assets are checked against.

Locations are per-world from here on. This document specifies one: the
forest. A second quantum-world biome gets its own reference and its own
section in this file when it exists — it does not inherit the forest's
palette by default, the same way the forest does not inherit the crossing's.


WHAT THE FOREST WORLD READS AS
--------------------------------

A wooded hillside at night. Cold sky, warm-dark ground, restrained colour.
The read comes from silhouette and layering, not from per-pixel detail — a
palette sample of the reference (quantized to 16 levels per channel, ~100k
sample points) came back 51.6% flat black and every other bucket in the top
twenty under RGB 96 on every channel. That is the whole visual argument:
almost nothing here is textured, and it doesn't need to be.

THE OUTPUT IS OUR OWN, NOT THE REFERENCE'S — notes/reference_observations.txt
already states the rule this document has to follow: informed by the
reference, not lifted from it. The palette below was designed to the same
character the sample measured — narrow value range, cool sky, warm-dark
ground — using this project's own values, not Noita's. Nowhere in this
pipeline does a pixel get copied out of test_location.jpg; resources/ is
gitignored specifically because that footage is reference, not material
(see .gitignore's note on this).


LAYER MODEL
-----------

notes/art_pipeline.txt specified three layers (Backdrop / Terrain / Props).
This reference needs four, because the trees are mid-ground — rooted at the
terrain's edge rather than drawn over the top of everything the way that
note's crossing-signal example was:

  1. Sky       - dusk gradient, banded, a handful of star pixels. Static per
                 scene. Parallax factor ~0.04.
  2. Mountains - flat cool-dark silhouette, one skyline shape. Parallax
                 factor ~0.15.
  3. Trees     - non-simulated sprites, colour-keyed, several per scene.
                 Parallax factor ~1 (they sit at roughly the terrain's own
                 depth, not further back).
  4. Terrain   - the simulated grid. Authored the way F4 already supports:
                 material map + albedo BMP through the existing legend.

Trees draw *before* the terrain's cell texture, not after. The cell texture
already composites over whatever is behind it wherever a cell is Empty
(V1) and paints solid wherever it isn't — so a tree drawn underneath it gets
free occlusion: the part of its trunk below the ground line disappears
behind authored dirt with no depth test and no new code path. That is the
one non-obvious ordering decision in this document and everything else
follows the existing composite rule.

Parallax offsets come from Camera::view_x()/view_y(), already exposed,
scaled by SCALE and each layer's own factor. No new coordinate system —
V8 already priced this at one SDL_RenderCopy per layer and that estimate
holds.


THE PALETTE'S INTENT
----------------------

ROADMAP.md's V5 entry names V2's existing decision as a rule to keep: cool
backdrop, warm world, saturation spent only where it has to be seen (Fire,
Water). This document keeps that rule and narrows it further for the forest
specifically:

  - Backdrop (sky, mountains): cool dark purple-blue. No warm colour
    anywhere in these two layers — that is what keeps them reading as
    *behind* everything else.
  - Terrain fill: warm near-black, not cool near-black. A shadowed dirt
    interior with no light on it still reads as earth rather than as void
    if it carries the faintest warmth, which a cool near-black does not.
  - The one bright thing in the terrain layer is the rim: a warm
    olive-gold line, one to two cells, on any surface that faces open air.
    Nothing else in the terrain is lit. This is authored per scene (see the
    dithering rule below), not computed by the engine — MATERIALS' colours
    are untouched, exactly as V6 requires.
  - Trees: desaturated green, cooler than the rim but warmer than the
    backdrop, so they sit legibly between the two in the depth stack without
    the eye needing motion parallax to place them.
  - Water keeps more saturation than anything else in the terrain layer,
    per the existing rule — it has to still read as water when the player
    digs into it, not just when it's compared side-by-side with dirt.

The full locked list, with the reasoning for each value, is
tools/pixel_art.py — that file is the palette now, this document is why it
looks the way it does. Do not add a colour to a scene that isn't in that
file; tools/validate_palette.py is what makes that checkable rather than
a good intention (V6).


THE DITHERING RULE
--------------------

V2 already learned the negative case: random per-cell jitter fights
hand-placed dither rather than adding to it, which is why color_jitter
stays out of anything this pipeline authors. The positive case, stated for
the first time here: transitions between two flat tones are stepped through
an ordered (Bayer-matrix) dither pattern, never a smooth interpolation and
never a hard edge with nothing between. tools/pixel_art.py's
`dither_mix` (over the `bayer_threshold` matrix) is the one
implementation; every generator script in tools/ calls it rather than
rolling its own gradient. Two places use it in this
scene: the sky's band transitions, and the one-cell hand-off between the
terrain's rim colour and its interior fill.

Dithering is for *transitions between authored flat regions*, not a
texture applied everywhere. Most of the terrain in this scene is a single
flat tone with no dither at all, because most of the reference is too — see
the palette-sample number above. Reach for dither at a boundary; do not
reach for it as a way to make a flat fill more interesting, which is the
same mistake color_jitter already was.


THE RIM ON MATERIALS THAT MOVE, MEASURED
------------------------------------------

The rim is baked per cell, so it travels with the cell - notes/
art_pipeline.txt accepted that consequence long ago for static terrain and
warned it "only breaks down under dramatic large-scale gradients". Sand and
Water are the case that acceptance did not cover: one slumps the moment the
sim starts, the other is a liquid. The worry was that the highlight would
scatter through the body of the material and read as noise.

Measured rather than argued, with tests/rim_probe.cpp - settle the authored
scene for 600 steps, then dig through the snowbank, breach the water
channel, and settle again:

                    SMEAR (adrift)    GAPS (unlit new surface)
  settled only      Sand  2.2%        Sand  15.3%
                    Water 0.0%        Water  0.0%
  after digging     Sand  4.8%        Sand  24.3%
                    Water 5.8%        Water  9.7%

THE WORRY WAS WRONG AND THE REAL EFFECT IS THE OTHER COLUMN. Highlight
genuinely adrift inside the material peaks at about 6% even after the
material is dug through - it does not become noise, because a cell that
moves almost always ends up on a surface again or directly under one. What
does happen is that movement exposes *new* top edge that carries no
highlight: a quarter of the settled sand surface after a dig.

That is kept deliberately, because it is information rather than an
artifact: undisturbed ground has a clean rim and ground the player has torn
up has a broken one, which is what disturbed earth should look like. The
decision would change if GAPS ever applied to terrain nobody touched.

The alternative, if it ever does bother someone: recompute the rim per frame
in the renderer over surface cells, rather than baking it at author time.
That is a real option and it is deliberately NOT taken now - a 6% number
does not buy a new per-frame pass, and it would sit uncomfortably close to
V7's "light must never become a simulation input" boundary.

Note the probe's first version reported 33% smear at t=0, before a single
step ran, because it counted the authored dither band under each rim cell as
damage. The band is about a third of all rim cells by construction. A metric
that indicts a feature before the feature has done anything is worth
double-checking; this one nearly argued for ripping out something that turned
out to be fine.


WHAT IS DRAWN VS WHAT IS SIMULATED
-------------------------------------

Unchanged from notes/art_pipeline.txt's original split, restated because V4
and V8 both depend on it: if a region of the scene exercises a system —
powder piling, structural collapse, liquid levelling, fire spreading — it
belongs in the terrain layer, authored through the material map, and it
stays exactly as laid out in generate_test_scene.py's fixture (the snowbank
slope, the fence posts, the bridge over the pit, the water channel, the
sleepers). Nothing about this document changes that layout, only its
colours. Everything else — sky, mountains, trees — exercises no system and
is drawn, non-simulated, and cannot be dug, ignited, or displaced.
