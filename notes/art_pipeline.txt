Art Pipeline / First Test Scene
================================

Plan for authoring the first pixel art scene and making the existing
materials and player read as part of it. Reference target: detailed snowy
Japanese railway-crossing scenes, roughly 640x400, cold desaturated
blue-grey palette with warm sodium and signal accents, heavy ordered
dithering in the sky and snow.

Nothing here is started until Short Term and Medium Term in ROADMAP.md are
clear. This is a written-down plan, not a work queue.


THE KEY INSIGHT: THE ENGINE CAN ALREADY RENDER AUTHORED ART
------------------------------------------------------------

Element carries a per-cell colour (element.h), and every move preserves it:
swap_elements swaps whole Elements and re-reads cells[idx].color into the
pixel buffer (grid.cpp), and drop_component falls through that same swap.

So a hand-painted stone slab keeps its hand-painted pixels while it falls
as a rigid body. Terrain drawn in an external editor reads as authored art
while it is static, and the moment it is disturbed it collapses carrying
its own shading. The engine is already ~95% of the way to this; it was
never designed for it, it just falls out of storing colour per cell and
moving cells by swapping.

The one gap: set_element overwrites colour with jittered_color(). It needs
a sibling that takes an explicit colour. That is the single API addition in
this entire plan.

Known consequence, accepted: shading is baked per cell, so a lit top edge
travels with the cell and can end up on the side of a fallen slab. At this
scale it reads as material texture, which is what Noita looks like. It only
breaks down under dramatic large-scale gradients painted into terrain --
so do not paint those into the simulated layer.


THIS FORKS A DECISION ALREADY IN THE ROADMAP
---------------------------------------------

Engineering Notes proposes shrinking Element to ~2 bytes by deriving colour
from the material table plus a per-cell jitter seed, and calls it the first
thing to try if the worst case has to come down.

That optimisation and authored per-cell colour are mutually exclusive.

The call: authoring wins. It is the entire visual pillar, and the note
itself says to spend the optimisation only when a scenario the *game*
produces goes over budget -- which has not happened. But this needs to be
struck deliberately rather than discovered later, and the RNG-cost item is
untouched and remains available as the cheap win instead.


LAYER MODEL
-----------

Most of what makes the references beautiful is in things that would never
be simulated anyway. Three layers:

  Backdrop  - dusk gradient, mountain, treeline, train car, power lines.
              Static image behind the cell texture. Not simulated.
  Terrain   - snowbank, embankment, rocks, fence posts, sleepers, water
              channel. Cells, with authored colour. Simulated.
  Props     - crossing signal, signs, barrier, chain-link fence.
              Drawn on top. Not simulated.

Backdrop and props cost one SDL_RenderCopy each.

Non-obvious prerequisite: Empty is currently opaque black (0xFF000000), so
the cell texture would paint over the backdrop. Empty must become
0x00000000 and the texture needs SDL_SetTextureBlendMode(BLENDMODE_BLEND).
Two lines, affects no physics, but nothing composites until it is done.


SCENE FORMAT
------------

Two same-size images per scene: a MATERIAL MAP and an ALBEDO. The material
map uses flat legend colours, one per ElementType. The albedo is the actual
art. Load both; each pixel becomes paint(x, y, type_from_legend,
colour_from_albedo).

Why two files rather than one: the art can be repainted without touching
the physics layout, and the layout can change without a repaint. Encoding
the material in an alpha channel is cleverer and miserable to author in
every editor that exists.

Use BMP via SDL_LoadBMP, not PNG. Zero new dependencies -- it is in core
SDL2, which is already fetched. PNG means pulling in SDL_image or zlib, and
a 640x400 BMP is about 750 KB for a test fixture. Aseprite, GIMP and
Photoshop all export BMP. Revisit only if the art pipeline genuinely needs
PNG.

Loading must not break the headless rule. src/physics/ stays SDL-free, so
the loader in src/scene/ takes plain (type[], colour[]) buffers and writes
to the grid, and a ~15-line SDL-side function in main.cpp turns a BMP into
those buffers. Scene loading then stays testable headlessly from an
in-memory buffer, the same as everything else in the project.


THE SCENE IS A TEST FIXTURE WEARING ART
----------------------------------------

This is what keeps it on the right side of Scope Discipline. A snowy
railway crossing built from the references also exercises every system
shipped so far:

  snowbank on a slope       -> powder piling, and the player's 2-cell
                               step-up over uneven powder
  wooden fence posts        -> dig the base, watch a structure collapse
                               while keeping its shape
  concrete platform, gap
  underneath                -> the support flood fill, and an L-piece
                               keeping its corner
  water channel             -> liquid levelling, density, player falling
                               through
  sleepers beside the water -> ignite wood, watch water douse it to steam
  ledges at mixed heights   -> jump arc tuning

If the scene does not exercise a system, it is decoration and belongs in
the backdrop layer instead.

Author at 640x400. Matches the references, and is PIXEL_SCALE 2 in a
1280x800 window. 256,000 cells -- half of what grid_bench already measures,
so comfortably inside budget. It is exactly one screen, so it does not
pre-empt the camera decision in Medium Term; when the camera lands, the
same loader handles a larger-than-screen world with no changes to it.


MAKING THE PLAYER BELONG
-------------------------

The body is a 4x8 white rectangle. The reference character is a
recognisable human around 24px tall. A person cannot be drawn in 4x8.

Decouple the sprite from the hitbox. Keep collision at 4x8 -- it is tuned
to the physics and 18 tests stand on it -- and draw a ~10x14 sprite
anchored to the box's bottom-centre with an offset. Standard practice,
touches no physics, breaks no tests, and puts the player at roughly the
reference's proportion of screen height.


MAKING SAND / WOOD / STONE BELONG
----------------------------------

Once terrain is authored, MATERIALS colours only govern brush-placed cells
and REACTION PRODUCTS -- wood that catches fire, water that becomes steam.
So there is a seam: authored scene-coloured wood burns into generic orange
fire. For fire that is arguably correct, since it should be the bright
accent in a cold scene. It is still why the palette pass matters.

Current colours are saturated primaries -- Water #4444FF, Sand #EEDD82 --
and will read as a debug view against the reference art no matter what else
is done. Retune each base colour into the scene palette.

Cut color_jitter hard. Sand's 18 is a lot of random noise, and authored
pixel art uses deliberate ordered dithering. Random per-cell jitter
actively fights hand-placed dither patterns rather than adding to them.

One tempting one-liner: Snow is a Powder row with lower density than Sand.
A single table entry, and the single most on-theme material for these
references.


PHASING
-------

  0  Grid::paint(x, y, type, colour) + test           ~10 lines
  1  Scene format + headless loader in src/scene/     ~120 lines
  2  BMP -> buffers in main.cpp, load at startup      ~30 lines
  3  Transparent Empty + backdrop render              ~10 lines
  4  Palette and jitter pass on MATERIALS             table only
  5  Player sprite decoupled from hitbox              ~40 lines
  6  Props overlay, Snow material                     optional

Phases 0-3 give a loadable authored scene. 4-5 are what make it cohere.

ALL SIX PHASES ARE NOW SCHEDULED IN ROADMAP.md. This note is the reasoning;
the roadmap is the task list, and where the two disagree the roadmap wins.

  0-2  ->  F4 in Foundations. Built and closed.
  3-6  ->  V1-V4 in Engine & Visual Depth, which sits between Foundations
           and Medium Term.

Both halves left Presentation & Tooling rather than being built while still
filed behind that section's "nothing starts here yet" rule - see the note on
that in VISION.md's Scope Discipline. Phases 3-6 moved for a reason this
note did not anticipate: the visual design became a stated selling point in
VISION.md rather than a finish applied after the loop worked, which is what
promoted them out of "presentation, later."

One ordering change the roadmap makes and this note does not: phase 6's Snow
is sequenced after heat lands (E2), because with a temperature in the engine
snow melting is a thermal row rather than a hand-written special case.

Per-cell emissive lighting is not in this note at all and is the next visual
item after V1-V4 - gated on there being authored art worth lighting.


DELIBERATELY DEFERRED
----------------------

The pixel art EDITOR. ROADMAP.md is right that it is a second application
with its own UI, file format and edit loop, and it is the exact seductive
side-build Scope Discipline warns about. An external editor plus this
loader covers authoring until art volume is measurably the bottleneck.

Note that phases 0-2 are not part of that deferral and are not wasted work:
a scene loader is a hard prerequisite for Quantum Worlds in Medium Term,
which needs some way to get a level into the grid regardless of whether the
level is authored or generated.
